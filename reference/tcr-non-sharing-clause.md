# TCR Non-Sharing Clause — Privacy Policy Requirement

Carriers (T-Mobile, AT&T, Verizon) and TCR's automated screening look for
a specific non-sharing paragraph in every A2P 10DLC Privacy Policy. The
exact wording matters — paraphrasing reduces approval rates and can
trigger automated rejections.

## The verbatim clause

```
No mobile information will be shared with third parties/affiliates for
marketing/promotional purposes. Information sharing to subcontractors in
support services, such as customer service, is permitted. All other use
case categories exclude text messaging originator opt-in data and consent;
this information will not be shared with any third parties.
```

## Why each part matters

The clause does three things in sequence:
1. Affirmatively declares no marketing-purpose data sharing
2. Carves out an exception for legitimate support subcontractors (which
   carriers expect — most operators DO use third-party customer service)
3. Explicitly excludes opt-in data and consent from any sharing

Versions that omit any of the three parts get flagged. Restructuring the
clause for "readability" is also flagged because TCR's screening is
pattern-matching on specific phrasings.

## When this comes up

Use this when:
- The user asks "what's the non-sharing clause?"
- The user is drafting or auditing a Privacy Policy
- A campaign was rejected for "missing PP non-sharing language" or
  similar
- The user is being told their PP "doesn't mention selling/sharing
  correctly"

## Common mistakes to flag

- Privacy Policy says "we may share with marketing partners" anywhere
  → guaranteed rejection. Suggest removing that language entirely.
- Privacy Policy is missing the verbatim clause → suggest adding it as
  a standalone paragraph in the SMS / Text Messaging section.
- Privacy Policy paraphrases the clause ("we don't sell your data") →
  not enough. Suggest replacing with the verbatim version.
